# Temperate map tile parity: OpenTTD 16.0-RC1

**Reference:** OpenTTD 16.0-RC1, commit [`07b339c`](https://github.com/OpenTTD/OpenTTD/tree/07b339c0aaeb0a87be9490b2f1c3f17c6b00b837).
**Goal:** Ship the complete stock Temperate map before moving chronologically through the larger content timeline. Other climates, NewGRFs and equivalent isometric sprites are not part of this denominator. Native 3D models need not look identical to the 2D artwork, but they do need to convey the same form, function and state.

On this checklist, a **tile** includes playable subtypes, footprints, geometries and meaningful visible states, not just the top-level class or one model per category. The upstream [`TileType` enum](https://github.com/OpenTTD/OpenTTD/blob/16.0-RC1/src/tile_type.h#L48-L60) has 11 classes. LuanTTD has some native representation for all 11; that does not close the gaps below.

## What the rocks and rapids actually are

- **Inland rock** is `ClearGround::Rocks`. In [`GenerateClearTile`](https://github.com/OpenTTD/OpenTTD/blob/16.0-RC1/src/clear_cmd.cpp#L351-L466), walks of rocks can start on land or coast and can enter clear sea or cross a river without replacing the river. The native RC1 pass was compared with the compiled upstream functions on 12 complete rasters, including terminal RNG state.
- **Open-water and coastal rock** are `WaterTileType::ClearRocks` and `CoastRocks`. Both still count as water in the appropriate visual direction, but [have no ship track bits](https://github.com/OpenTTD/OpenTTD/blob/16.0-RC1/src/water_cmd.cpp#L1419-L1431). The [native test](../tests/rocky_water_tiles.lua) checks generation, checkpoint, worker projection, navigation, flood/dry-out and explicit clearance. Docks, depots and buoys clear the occupied rock tiles at the source cost; the dock approach check accepts flat rocky water, but no ship can use that tile as a berth. A seeded [depot-to-dock ferry simulation](../tests/ship_preview_run.lua) crosses only outside a rocky barrier and repeats after reload. A [native company replay](../tests/stock_rocky_coast_ferry_native.lua) builds docks and buys an FFP ferry on an ordinary generated 256×256 map. Six untouched sea rocks block the shorter route; the vessel takes the offshore route, carries fare-paying passengers, and keeps earning after reopen.
- **Rocky-looking rapids** are not a third rock state. [`DrawRiverWater`](https://github.com/OpenTTD/OpenTTD/blob/16.0-RC1/src/water_cmd.cpp#L926-L961) draws a flat river or one of four inclines plus river-edge art. Keep river rock/foam presentation directional and inside the exposed channel. Do not assign the rocky-sea flag or substitute sea rules for an inclined river. Look for wet faces protruding through the banks, especially at a sloped mouth joining flat sea.
- **Lighthouse rock clusters** are a separate generation step. [`TryBuildLighthouseNearTile`](https://github.com/OpenTTD/OpenTTD/blob/16.0-RC1/src/object_cmd.cpp#L752-L780) first finds a plain coast candidate and then walks rock clusters from adjacent shore using nibbles of one random word for the walk lengths. RC1 runs both [town and coast lighthouse searches](https://github.com/OpenTTD/OpenTTD/blob/16.0-RC1/src/object_cmd.cpp#L788-L864), in that order. The port uses the live completed town/industry board and preserves spiral order and RNG handoff; [compiled source comparisons](../tests/scenery_object_rc1_upstream.py) matched 18 complete boards.
