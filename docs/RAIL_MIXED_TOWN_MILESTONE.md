# Mixed RC1 Coach and Mail consists

Reference: pinned OpenTTD 16.0-RC1.

## Design contract

- Use pinned OpenTTD 16.0-RC1 `economy.cpp` `ReserveConsist`, `LoadUnloadVehicle`, `LoadUnloadStation`, `CargoPayment`; yard ownership and stable ordered car identities come from the normal stock vehicle catalog/assembly and Native Run dispatcher. Keep one ordered train entry, one movement clock, one station-loading timer including overhang, and one money-bearing per-visit CargoPayment.
- A capacity-bearing purchased body has its own stock ID, immutable yard body ID, current physical tile, town cargo ID, stock capacity/slice/age phase, FIFO packet list and reservations. Locomotives in this gate have no cargo. Station host supplies two independent GoodsEntry queues and ratings, but the existing one-house Mail source and one-house passenger source remain their sole allocators; do not emit from the vehicle.
- Model `full_load_amount`, `reservation_left`, `cargo_full`, `cargo_not_full` per cargo type. A non-full body prevents `Full load all`; `Full load any` requires every body of at least one cargo type to be full. A later loading attempt changes that cargo's slice mask only. Only finalized real moving-body deliveries increment company cash. Aggregate fare once per visit, with preserved per-cargo receipt and feeder-share breakdowns.
- Mixed service derives automatically from the stock art and mounted service, not a feature setting. Native proof focuses one ordinary modeled locomotive with two/three wagons in purchase order, both #27 and #28 present. No DMU/push-pull/mid-route refits, industrial integration, Mail-only expansion, or change to stock all-passenger and single Mail availability. Manual town routing and live stock order flags remain the ordinary controls.
- Proof includes distinct yard-native examples: Kirby + Carriage + Mail Van and Kirby + Mail Van + Carriage + Mail Van. Use real platforms, purchase, assembly, operating expense, two process/SQLite restarts, genuine house cargo and per-part visual IDs across reversal. Unit checks hit the failure modes of full-any versus full-all, transfer/no unload, ready order timing, independent mass, and no duplicate fare. Actual headless worker drive and Linux client rendered mesh swap must be checked before handoff.

## Play and test

No feature setting is required. The ordinary depot offers mixed service when the installed game has the original stock Passenger Carriage, Mail Van and a compatible modeled locomotive, plus the shared owned rail and town Mail service. Buy a supported conventional locomotive and two or three stock vehicles #27 (Passenger Carriage) and #28 (Mail Van) with at least one of each. Couple them in either order in the depot Train Yard and assign two sufficiently long built town platforms in the ordinary rail order editor. The order panel reports the two types separately.

- Lua unit tests verify a train with two carriages and a Mail Van cannot leave on one full carriage under Full Load Any, can leave on full Mail when the carriages are not all full, and cannot leave that way under Full Load All. They also verify independent last load slices, one station cadence, separate waiting cargo and feeder shares, and no payment for transfer.
- Two actual Luanti worker formations were driven through construction, individual stock purchase and yard coupling, operations, paid delivery, and two native SQLite reopens per run. Both continued saved part-level cargo/age and a partly unloaded, unpaid station visit, settled once, preserved exact cash/revenue and original body IDs through reversal, and exercised live order changes. Carriage+Mail settled $1,033 (40 people/$488 and 30 bags/$545); Mail+Carriage+Mail settled $1,576 (40 people/$486 and 60 bags/$1,090).
- A Linux Luanti client mounted the original models and rendered the authority-produced loading and post-reversal body snapshots for both formations (four frames). The screenshot fixture replays the real projected bodies on display tracks; it does not drive a second economy or substitute for the native worker route.
- The native ordinary generated campaign boot, using production initialization and its actual depot query after worker handoff, offered a mixed Kirby + Carriage + Mail train with no settings change. Missing stock art, absent owned rail/Mail service, two engines, DMUs, dual-head engines and excess wagons are refused by the capability and formation tests.
- Native unchanged all-passenger service settled $192 with one carriage after a SQLite restart, and ordinary single-Mail rail settled $576 for 30 bags competing with a road Mail truck for the same houses after reload.
- Repeat with `lua5.1 tests/stock_mixed_town_rail_service.lua`, `bash tests/stock_mixed_town_rail_live_native.sh`, and `BAYLINE_MIXED_FORM=mail-coach-mail bash tests/stock_mixed_town_rail_live_native.sh`. The two native runs print evidence roots; pass their `world` directories in that order to `tests/stock_mixed_town_rail_render.sh` for the optional Linux X11 screenshot run.

This gate does not claim unrestricted RC1 consist parity: more than three town wagons, powered/dual-headed mixed sets, mixed town+industry consists, and refitting bodies in place are out of scope. The game’s existing station-geometry, payment-age, and station rating kernels apply. Preserve the sealed preview unchanged; new mixed worlds belong on this separate branch.
