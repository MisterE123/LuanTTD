# Temperate rocky water and rapids

OpenTTD 16.0-RC1 `src/water_cmd.cpp` draws `ClearRocks` over the
usual water class and `CoastRocks` over the usual shoreline; both use
the slope's rock overlay. The native art uses the pinned shore masks
`1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9,11,12,13,14,23,27,29,30` and level open sea.
It does not put rocks on all water or change the shape, waterline,
coast clearance, or any world-generation probabilities.

- **Rocky shore:** the ordinary shingle and bank use the same vertices
  and texture pixels. Ground-matched boulders at the toe straddle
  the waterline on the existing shore slopes.
- **Open-water rocks:** low, tapered skerries sit among separate rocks
  with crests below mean water level. Waves expose the shallow rocks
  first. The sea shader and the navigation rules are unchanged.
- **River rapids:** enclosed rock volumes rise from beneath the
  four stock river inclines. Short-lived foam particles originate
  just behind the rocks and fade as they travel downslope. Only
  nearby players receive them. The flat river sections and inclines
  share a fixed geometry to avoid opening a gap at the gradient
  joins; their directional flow textures still animate. No special
  upstream rocky-river tile class or hydrology changes.

The OBJ assets, palettes, and foam sprite are generated by
`tools/build_stock_rocky_water_art.py`. Its `--blend` mode writes the
editable `stock_rocky_water.blend` with every rock as a separately
named mesh and review-only water datums. No original OpenTTD or
OpenGFX sprites, pixels, or meshes were copied. Tests:
`python3 tests/stock_rocky_water_art.py`; with a graphical Luanti
installation, `BAYLINE_NATIVE_ROCKY_WATER=1 bash tests/coastal_sheet_native.sh`
(run again with `BAYLINE_NATIVE_WAVES=0` to compare).
